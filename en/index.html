What is imported, and what is not?

The scan result lists every kind of record in your export, with how many there are and from which years. Whatever you leave switched on is imported.

Some tables cannot be imported. The scan result names each one and says why:

  • Apple Health has no data type that means the same thing.
  • Apple Health stores wrist temperature as a change from your baseline, not as an absolute temperature, so the values cannot be converted honestly.
  • Apple Health accepts this type only from Apple Watch.
  • Importing daily totals on top of your workouts and steps would count the same activity twice.

These stay in your export file. Nothing is lost; Apple Health simply has no matching place for them.

How do I check the result in Apple Health?

Open the Health app. On Summary, tap Show All Health Data to see every kind of data you have, or tap Search / Browse and type what you are looking for, for example sleep or heart rate.

Something looks wrong. How do I remove it?

There are three ways, from the simplest to the most thorough. Removing records does not touch your export file, and you can import again afterwards.

1Undo one import, in this app

On the first screen, open Past imports and undo and undo the import. Everything it added is removed in one step. Try this first.

Only records added by this import are removed. Data from your Apple Watch, other apps and other imports is not touched. You can import the same file again later.

2Remove one kind of data, or single records, in the Health app

Open that kind of data in the Health app, scroll to the bottom and tap Data Sources & Access. Under Data Sources, tap Sampple: the list now holds only what this app wrote. Tap Edit, then remove single records, or all of them with Delete All.

How long does an import take, and what if it is interrupted?

It depends on how much there is and on the iPhone. Before you start, the app shows an estimate worked out from the number of records. While it imports, it shows how much is left, judged from how fast it has gone so far. Both are ranges, not promises.

Keep the app open while it imports. The screen stays on, so plug in the charger for a long history. If you lock the iPhone, the import waits and goes on by itself when you unlock it: Apple Health cannot be read while the iPhone is locked.

You can pause at any time. If the app is closed or the iPhone restarts in the middle, open the app again: it offers to continue from where it stopped. Nothing is added twice, and undoing the import still removes all of it.

After a large import, Apple Health may need some time before it shows all of the history.

The times look a few hours off

Times are imported exactly as recorded. The Health app shows them in the time zone your iPhone is in now, so nights recorded far away can look shifted. The data itself is correct.

Why were some hours left out?

If an Apple Watch or this iPhone already recorded the same hours, the older records for those hours are left out so steps, sleep and workouts are not counted twice.

It is the switch Skip time Apple Health already has under More options on the scan result.

About heart rate variability

Samsung watches measure heart rate variability almost only while you sleep. The export holds two figures for every reading, and Apple Health has a place for each: Recovery HRV and Heart Rate Variability.

What is imported is your history from before you switched watches. It hardly overlaps in time with what an Apple Watch records, so single days and weeks are not affected. What you may see is a step at the date you switched, in the month and year views and in long-term trends, because two watches do not measure in quite the same way.

That step shows more in Heart Rate Variability than in Recovery HRV. So Heart Rate Variability is only imported if you turn it on.

Recovery HRV exists from iOS 27 on; an older iOS has no place for it. After updating, import the same file again and only what is missing is added.

Samsung stores a reading every 30 seconds, each covering the five minutes before it. One reading per five minutes is imported, so that no stretch of time is counted ten times.

I used two Samsung accounts

If you used more than one Samsung account, export each and add them together.

Importing the same export again adds nothing new, so it is safe to retry after an interruption.

What is the 1-Year Pass, and what happens when it ends?

One payment opens everything for one year from the moment you buy it: all of your history, every source the app supports, as often as you like. What is added to the app during that year is included.

It does not renew by itself. After a year it ends; nothing more is charged and there is nothing to cancel. If you buy again while a pass is still running, the new year is added at its end.

When it has ended, nothing is taken away. What you imported stays in Apple Health, and scanning, previewing, the sample file, the free part, undoing imports and removing what this app added all keep working. Only importing more than the free part needs a pass again.

New iPhone, or the app was reinstalled: how do I get my pass back?

The pass belongs to your Apple Account, not to one iPhone. On the screen that offers the pass, tap Restore purchase. You are not charged again, and the pass ends on the same day as before.

Can I get my money back?

Refunds are handled by Apple: go to reportaproblem.apple.com, sign in and choose the purchase. After a refund the pass ends at once and imports are limited to the free part again. What is already in Apple Health stays there.

The transfer stopped, or the zip file cannot be opened

Both phones must be on the same Wi‑Fi network, and this iPhone must stay on its receiving screen with the display on. Then send the zip file again from the other phone.

If the zip file cannot be opened, make it again on the Samsung phone: leave the format on Zip, without a password and without splitting it by size.

What happens to my data?

Your export is read on this iPhone and written to Apple Health on this iPhone. It is never uploaded.

The app has no account, no analytics, no advertising and no server.

Receiving from another phone uses only your own Wi-Fi network, directly between the two phones.

If you write to us, we receive what you choose to send: your message and, if you leave that switch on, the technical details shown to you before sending.
